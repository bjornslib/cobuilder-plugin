The shape of it

Findings

F1

Three HTML conventions, three destinations

architect:review writes paired reports to docs/architecture/review/. export_artifact.py inlines a bundle into exports/pr-N.html. collaborate-with-user writes .lavish/<slug>.html. All three build a self-contained page for a person. None shares a template, a palette, or a destination.

F2

The viewer is the largest surface and the only silent one

Eleven artifacts converge there, tied for the most of any surface, and none can be answered. A person reads a whole pull request as a story and has nowhere to put a reaction.

F3

The review reports are the sharpest case

They are the longest human-facing output in the system, they carry severity ratings and a phased remediation plan, and they land on a file opened from disk — no server, no URL, no way for the reader to respond.

F4

Divergent exploration persists nothing

Stage 3 runs isolated frames and a critic that scores every candidate, flags traps with reasoning, and stars non-obvious survivors. Only the survivors reach the ADR. This happened in this session: five frames and a critic pass produced material that exists only in the transcript.

F5

Two artifacts hide from a reader on purpose

The rubrics are written for the validator and withheld from the implementer, because an implementer that reads the answer key writes code shaped to it. Every other artifact wants more readers, not fewer.

F6

Most of the viewer is read indirectly

Seven of its eleven artifacts are compiled before a person sees them. Only the images, the audio, and the two Mermaid sources are viewed as themselves. Filter to Directly viewed to see how little of the surface is what it appears to be.

Open questions

  1. Should the three HTML conventions in F1 converge under cobuilder-artifact, or stay separate because they answer to different readers? Converging them is a real refactor, not a rename.
  2. Should the viewer gain a return path? It is the biggest reading surface in the system and the only large one with no way to reply. Publishing it as an Artifact already provides comments — the question is whether that should be the default rather than an extra step.
  3. Should stage 3 write its critic output to disk? An exploration.json beside intent.json would preserve the traps and the scores, at the cost of one more file in every design directory.
