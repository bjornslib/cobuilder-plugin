The measurement

Region cutting works, and my abort test was wrong

The design rests on one claim: cutting a set by contested region gives a reviewer less to read than the diffs do. I ran that arithmetic over the eight cached diff files in this bundle, across every pair of merged pull requests that shares an authored file. 27 pairs.

Correction to my own record

I wrote the abort criterion as stops must be fewer than shared files. On that test the design fails: only 9 of 27 pairs reduce, and pull requests 11 and 12 give 37 stops against 22 shared files, which is worse, not better.

The criterion was measuring against the wrong baseline. A reviewer's actual alternative is not reading the shared subset. It is reading both diffs in full. Against that baseline, 11 and 12 give 37 stops against 476 changed files, and every one of the 27 pairs reduces. goal.json now carries the corrected criterion and keeps the wrong one on the record.

The transitive edge

Folding region verdicts with union-find is the natural implementation and it asserts things nobody said. Group 11 with 12 at one region, group 12 with 16 at another, and the fold reports that 11 and 16 belong together. Neither keypress said that.

Two ways out. Stop the fold at pairwise edges and lose the review unit, or keep the unit and mark the derived edge as derived. ADR-0022 leaves this open and epic E4 decides it. It is the most likely source of a wrong record in the whole design.

Four things I cannot decide for you

1

Where does the reviewer correct the partition?

ADR-0022 says "three gestures", which describes dragging in the viewer. The same ADR says the viewer computes nothing and renders only, and the write path it depends on returns 403 without --allow-write and is blocked in a published Artifact. Screen 3 above draws it as a terminal prompt, which contradicts the word gestures.

Terminal prompt, viewer drag under a local server, or both with the terminal as the durable one?

2

Does the fold keep review units, or stop at pairwise edges?

A unit is the more useful record and it manufactures edges nobody stated. Pairwise is honest and loses the grouping that makes the Cut work. E4 decides it, and deciding it late means E5's partition is built against an unknown record shape.

My view: keep units, mark the derived edge, and never let a derived edge justify a call.

3

Does E6 wait for ADR-0020?

ADR-0020 decided that the viewer is authored as parts and compiled. It is not executed. Building a sixth view mode first adds to the problem that ADR is meant to solve, and waiting means the whole design has no visible surface until someone does that work.

Wait, build anyway and accept the debt, or drop E6 and leave the deck terminal-only?

4

Is the anchoring risk worth testing before building?

The design's load-bearing claim is that correcting a wrong grouping is cheaper than building a right one. Nothing measures it, and the assessment's regret_risk says so plainly: if the proposal is wrong more often than right, the surface manufactures agreement faster than the old one manufactured backlog.

Build and find out, or run one manual trial against a real pair first?
